Up/Down Evidence Report

Every claim from today's research, with the chart and the numbers behind it. All figures come from the repo's own data files (598 finished windows, 1.49 million public fills, and our bot's own order-book readings). Nothing here involves real money.

1. The markets resolve on a Chainlink 60-second average, and Binance only approximates it

strong

Polymarket publishes an official "price to beat" and final price for every window. "Final ≥ price to beat" reproduces all 598 results. Rules built on Binance prices get it wrong in 2% to 16% of windows, which is exactly where a bot built on Binance loses.

What it means: the old bot (v1/v2) used Binance and lost . The new bot reads the exact Chainlink feed and the official price to beat, so its view of the outcome is the same as the market's.

2. A 15-minute window and the 5-minute window that ends with it share the same final price

strong

Both compare the same final average against different starting prices. One share of the right side of each therefore pays at least $1, and $2 when the final lands between the two starting prices. Checked on every finished pair with official numbers.

What it means: this is a structural fact, not a prediction. Whether it makes money depends on the two markets pricing the pair inconsistently; the bot now logs every such pair and only paper-trades it when the asks add up to less than it is worth.

3. In the last two minutes the crowd overpays for favourites and underpays underdogs

strong

Every buy in 598 windows, grouped by the price paid and the time left, scored against the real result and charged the full taker fee. Blue cells made money, red cells lost.

What it means: buying the favourite at 50-90¢ with 30-120 s left lost 6-9% per dollar; buying the underdog at 10-30¢ in the same minutes returned +22% to +32%. But see the next section before reading that as a strategy.

4. The underdog profit is a handful of windows, not a repeatable edge

dead

Same fills (cheap side, 30-120 s left), now summed per window and sorted. The total is positive only because of a few windows where informed traders caught a late reversal in size.

What it means: a rule that buys every cheap underdog late collects the many small losses and rarely the rare big win, which went to whoever was fastest. Kept running as a control only.

What it means: the repeatable winners do not predict prices. One buys sure things at 97-99¢ in size (+0.9% per dollar, 324 windows, no losses). One never buys and sells both sides above 50¢ as a fee-free market maker (+4.2% per dollar, 231 windows). A few buy 1-2 minutes before the end on the exact feed and win about 90% of their windows. The largest losers bet "Up" 70-76% of the time and win a third of their bets.

6. The riskless "buy both sides under $1" trade is swept before we can see it

strong

Our bot reads both order books every 2-3 seconds in the last minutes. If Up and Down could ever be bought for less than $1 together, that reading would show it.

What it means: taking riskless gaps from the book is a speed game we cannot win from a polling script. The maker side of the same trade (selling both sides for more than $1) is the one worth replaying with our pricing.

7. On the exact feed our model is honest, but the market is still sharper

early

First afternoon of the new bot (200 windows with Chainlink-fed readings). Lower prediction error is better. An earlier version of this page, built on 27 windows, said the model beat the market inside the last minute; with ten times the data that is no longer true at any horizon. The model's confidence levels do hold up (second chart), so it is not wrong, just coarser than a crowd that also watches order flow.

What it means: the feed is now known to be the oracle itself: the feed's average at the window end equals the official final price exactly in 1,638 of 1,638 windows, and at the start equals the price to beat in 639 of 639. Our view of the outcome is therefore exact; what we lack is the crowd's speed and its read of order flow. Full-night paper results by strategy: .

8. Overnight verdict: three of five rules are dead, two are thin and speed-limited

strong

A full night on the clean feed (about 1,750 windows, 8 coins, 147,000 Chainlink-fed book readings). Every figure is paper: the bot assumed it could buy the displayed ask at the displayed size the moment it saw it.

What it means: in these markets the only money is in speed. The sweeper's trade (buy the sure side at 98-99¢) returns about zero for us even when our model agrees (), because the price already carries the jump risk. Lock-in survives as a thin edge of perhaps +2-4% per trade on 1-4 trades an hour, half of which depends on reaching a quote inside two seconds; the 15m/5m pairs are riskless but worth cents. Nothing here justifies real money yet; the next test is measuring how long those quotes actually live, with a live order-book feed instead of polling.

9. Slow idea A: fading a sudden spike pays on average, as a lottery

early

Random sample of 53,394 closed long-dated markets (80 million trades). When a market suddenly trades X cents away from its own trailing-day average, we buy the opposite side 5-30 minutes later at the then price plus 1¢, and sell a day later minus 1¢ (or hold to resolution). No speed involved.

What it means: a real statistical effect, consistent across 2024-2026, but on a $224 account it would look like weeks of nothing and then one hit. Reasonable as a small, long-running component; not a source of daily gains.

10. Slow idea B: following the strong traders cannot be done slowly

dead

Wallets were graded on 25,044 markets that closed before May 2026 (at least 15 markets each, consistency measured by a t-statistic), then watched on 22,964 later markets. "Skilled" wallets (2,488) really do keep winning. The question is whether anything is left for someone who sees their trade and acts an hour later.

What it means: on Polymarket, information becomes price within minutes. The strong traders are strong because they are fast on news, not because they hold knowledge the market takes days to absorb.

11. Slow idea C: market-making in long-dated markets loses to everyone, not only to the sharp

dead

Replay on the same 22,964 markets: quote both sides around the last trade, $20 a side, get filled by the real prints that crossed the quote, mark at resolution. Split by who hit the quote.

What it means: together with the fast-market replay and the live pilot's loss, this is the third time market-making has failed for us, now across spreads, horizons and counterparties. Closed.
